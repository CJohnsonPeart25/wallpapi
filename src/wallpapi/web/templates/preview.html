{# The preview: a fixed frame over the **Batch** under Pico's overlay, opened by clicking a tile's thumbnail. One dialog for the whole page rather than one per tile: only one **Wallpaper** is ever being looked at. It sits outside `main` and outside anything htmx swaps, so the Alpine component on it lives as long as the page does and a tile swap underneath cannot take it away. **The image carries no source in the markup, and never gets one by binding.** `open` sets it by hand when a thumbnail is clicked and `closed` removes it again, so the full-resolution file is fetched from Wallhaven only when somebody asks to see it and a dismissed preview stops downloading (ADR 0003). The tile carries the URLs as data attributes, which cost nothing until read. The frame is one fixed size, three quarters of the screen, whatever shape the wallpaper is: every preview opens in the same place at the same size, so the eye never has to re-find it, and letterboxing is the price. Not Pico's card: a card resizes round its content and scrolls when it is too tall, which is the one thing a frame must not do. Pico still paints the overlay round it. A click on anything that is not a control dismisses it: the overlay round the frame, the image, the rail's gaps. Escape does the same natively. In fullscreen a click on the image leaves fullscreen instead, since the dialog is no longer what is on screen. The rail is the preview's own **Verdict** rail, below the image where it covers nothing; the two pills in the top corner are the way out to Wallhaven and fullscreen. The rail posts nothing of its own: it clicks the matching control on the tile behind, so there is one route, one swap and one marked state. After any swap the rail reads the tile's mark back, which is how the ring and the rail agree. Marking does not dismiss the preview. #}
